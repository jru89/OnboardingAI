# Mock Scenario: Draft a Client Update Email

**How to use this file:** Download it, then paste its contents (or upload the file itself) into your Claude Code session with a request like: *"Using the brief below, draft a short update email to the client. Keep it warm but professional, and don't promise a new date until I confirm it."*

There's no single right answer here — the point of this exercise is to practice giving Claude enough context to write something useful on the first try, then asking for revisions.

---

## Background Brief

You work at **Acme Fictional Co.**, a small design studio. You need to send a short update email to one of your longer-standing clients.

- **Client**: Riverbend Community Library (contact: **jane.doe@example.invalid**, Head Librarian)
- **What's changed**: The new library signage project ("Wayfinding Signs — Phase 2") is running about two weeks behind schedule. The delay is because the powder-coating vendor had a backlog, not because of anything the design team did wrong. The revised delivery estimate is **the last week of next month**, but that date is not confirmed yet — you're still waiting on the vendor to commit to it.
- **What you want the email to do**:
  1. Let Jane know about the delay before she hears it some other way.
  2. Briefly explain the reason (vendor backlog) without over-explaining or sounding defensive.
  3. Give her a realistic sense of timing, while being clear the new date is not yet locked in.
  4. Offer a quick call if she wants to talk it through.
- **Tone**: Friendly and direct — Riverbend has been a client for three years and the relationship is good. Avoid corporate-sounding filler ("We value your partnership...") and avoid over-apologizing.
- **Sign-off**: From **Sam Okafor**, Project Lead, Acme Fictional Co.

## Things to try in your Claude Code session

- Ask for a first draft, then ask Claude to make it shorter, or to make it warmer/more formal, and see how the revision changes.
- Try being vague on purpose (just say "write an email about the delay") and compare the result to what you get when you paste in the full brief above. Notice the difference context makes.
- If Claude invents a specific new delivery date instead of caveating it, that's a good moment to practice correcting it: "Don't state a firm date — say we're waiting on vendor confirmation."

*(Acme Fictional Co., Riverbend Community Library, Jane Doe, and Sam Okafor are all invented for this exercise. Any resemblance to a real company or person is coincidental.)*
